The Seventh-day Adventist English Church, Vellore, has been a pioneering

spiritual institution since 1947 , dedicated to nurturing young Christian leaders,

especially among students of Christian Medical College (CMC), Vellore.

From Dr. John Quinn, one of the earliest members, to present-day CMC

students, the church has provided a foundation in leadership, spiritual training,

and the art of self-discipline, preparing youth to serve as Christ’s ambassadors.

Over the decades, these students have gone on to serve across India, Pakistan,

Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Burma, Malaysia, and Singapore, establishing

Adventist hospitals, sanatoriums, and medical educational services, particularly

within the Southern Asia Division and Southern Asia Pacific Ocean Division — a legacy that has continued for over 79

years.

Today, alumni from this church are present on every continent, serving the

Lord in diverse and impactful ways. We invite your continued prayers and support as this church empowers a new generation to carry the mission of the

Gospel to the ends of the earth.

1. Medical Education Connection Begins (1945–1949)

1945 – Adventist-CMC Partnership Discussions

* The Southern Asia Division began negotiations with Christian Medical College (CMC), Vellore.
* Dr. Theodore R. Flaiz was appointed to develop a sponsorship program for Adventist medical students.
* Advertisements inviting Adventist students to pursue medical education at CMC were published.

1947 – First Adventist Medical Students

* CMC began its first co-educational medical program.
* Dr. John Quinn became the first known Adventist student admitted through the sponsorship program.

1948–1949 – Official Representation

* Adventist leaders and medical representatives regularly attended CMC council meetings.
* Dr. Thomas Thompson joined the CMC faculty as an Adventist representative.

1. Growth of the Adventist Student Community (1950–1957)

1950–1952

* Qualification criteria for Adventist-sponsored medical students were established.
* Elizabeth Fernando became one of the earliest Adventist women to enter the medical program.

1953 – Adventist Faculty Arrive

* Dr. Jacob and Dr. Ernestine Janzen joined CMC as missionary faculty.
* Sabbath worship services began meeting at the Mission Bungalow.
* Dr. John Quinn completed his medical studies and became one of the first Adventist graduates.

1953 – First Organized Worship Community

* Adventist students, faculty, and families regularly met for Sabbath worship, Bible study, communion services, and fellowship gatherings.

1954–1957

* Dr. Albert J. Patt and later Dr. Gordon Hadley arrived as faculty members.
* Dr. Patt became a key leader of the Adventist community at CMC.
* Additional Adventist specialists were sent by the General Conference to strengthen both CMC and the Adventist student fellowship.

⸻

1. Chitteri Branch Sabbath School Ministry (1954–1966)

1954

* Dr. Patt and six students started a Branch Sabbath School in Chitteri village.
* The village headman provided his family courtyard for weekly meetings.

Following Years

* Regular Sabbath School classes, health education, Bible stories, singing programs, and missionary activities were conducted.
* More than seventy children eventually participated.

Educational Impact

* Several village children were assisted in obtaining Adventist education.
* Financial aid was arranged for students to study at Adventist institutions such as Lowry Memorial College.

⸻

1. Organization of the Vellore Adventist Church (1958)

November 29, 1958

* The Vellore Adventist Church was officially organized.
* Twenty-three charter members formed the congregation.
* Worship services were held in the living room of Dr. Patt’s Maranatha Bungalow.
* English and Tamil Sabbath Schools operated simultaneously.
* Members were actively trained in church leadership and evangelism.

⸻

1. Expansion of Ministries and Student Leadership (1959–1965)

1959

* Prayer and Leadercraft training programs were conducted for Adventist students.

1960–1962

* Dr. Andrew Haynal joined CMC and became active in health evangelism and village outreach.
* Health education programs combined medical ministry with gospel outreach.

1963

* Membership reached approximately thirty-five people.
* Separate English and Tamil worship groups functioned regularly.
* Midweek prayer meetings, singing bands, literature ministry, and village Sabbath Schools became established ministries.

1964

* The church’s Sabbath School was recognized for its excellent organization.
* Medical students conducted evangelistic meetings in Chitteri village.
* Forty Bibles were distributed during outreach campaigns.

1965

* Twenty-one Adventist medical students had graduated from CMC by this time.
* Dr. Patt and Dr. Small were acknowledged for their significant contributions.

⸻

1. Evangelistic Growth and New Membership (1966–1968)

1966

* Evangelistic campaigns were conducted in Budoor village and surrounding areas.
* Several people from non-Christian backgrounds accepted baptism.
* Church membership reached approximately forty.

1967

* Adventist medical graduates assumed leadership positions throughout India.
* Additional Adventist medical specialists continued serving at CMC.

1968

* Adventist representatives participated in CMC association meetings.
* Adventist ophthalmologist Dr. John C. Abraham organized eye camps and cataract surgeries.
* Sixteen Adventist students were enrolled in medical training.

⸻

1. Retreats, Health Ministry, and Youth Development (1969–1972)

1969

* The first Adventist church retreat was organized at Horsley Hills.
* Medical students, staff, pastors, and alumni attended.

1970

* Dr. Theodore R. Flaiz completed fifty years of medical missionary service.
* Dr. L. A. Senseman represented Adventist health principles at national prohibition conferences attended by Indian leaders.

1970 – Youth Centre Established

* Land was acquired near Bagayam.
* A Youth Centre was built and dedicated for Sabbath School and Adventist Youth activities.

1971

* CMC hosted a major Evangelical Medical Institute.
* Adventist physicians and leaders taught health evangelism, physiology, counseling, nutrition, and family life.

1972

* Branch Sabbath Schools expanded into additional villages.
* Students and doctors traveled weekly to conduct children’s ministries and health education programs.
* Plans for a permanent church building accelerated.

⸻

1. Construction and Dedication of the Church Building (1973–1975)

1973

* Construction of a permanent church building began.
* The proposed church was designed to seat approximately 120 worshippers.
* Funds were raised through donations from members and supporters.

1974

* Adventist medical graduates continued village ministry and community service throughout South India.

1975 – Historic Dedication

* The Vellore Seventh-day Adventist Church building was officially dedicated.
* The church was designed by R. S. Senseman.
* Pastor C. P. Jonahs served as the church pastor.
* Membership had grown to approximately 120 believers.

⸻

Legacy of the Vellore SDA Church

The history of the Vellore Seventh-day Adventist Church is marked by:

* Early literature evangelism and Bible study ministries.
* A unique partnership with Christian Medical College, Vellore.
* Medical missionary service and health evangelism.
* Strong student leadership and discipleship.
* Village outreach through Branch Sabbath Schools.
* Growth from a small home fellowship in 1921 to an organized church with its own building by 1975.

The church’s development was shaped by dedicated pastors, physicians, students, missionaries, and lay members who combined the healing ministry of Christ with evangelism and education.
